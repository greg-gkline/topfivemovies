With *Finding Forrester*, director Gus Van Sant adheres to the old
adage: stick to what you're good at. In 1997, Van Sant rode an unlikely
horse by the name of *Good Will Hunting* straight into the winner's
circle known as box-office and critical success. In 2000, he tried to
recreate that success with Finding Forrester, a heart-warming tale of an
inner-city smarty-pants who doesn't want to own up to his intellectual
prowess because his basketball buddies will slag him for being a wuss. Or
something like that.

Mr. Smarty McSmart Guy quickly meets up with a somewhat aged author William
Forrester (played by Connery). Forrester agrees to help the young chap
improve his writing skills in order to succeed in his new private school --
on one condition: the boy can't reveal the relationship or the fact that he
knows Forrester at all.

Of course, like in any feel-good movie, troubles ensue -- at least in the
middle of the movie. Smart-Guy faces cheating accusations, Forrester faces
a crippling fear of leaving the musty confines or his apartment, etc. Rest
assured, however, that through Forrester's gritty Scottish wits and
Smarty's street-wise irreverance, trouble has met its match.

Don't get me wrong, this is not a bad movie. In fact, on its own merits,
it's not too bad at all. However, I know I would have liked it a lot more
had I not seen (and enjoyed) *Good Will Hunting*. Stylistically,
however, the two are just too similar. And, no, going into it I didn't
know the director was Van Sant. Two minutes in, I figured it out.

So, if you enjoyed *Good Will Hunting*, go rent *Finding
Forrester*. If you've never seen *GWH*, go rent *Finding
Forrester* and enjoy it without having your experience pre-tainted by
association.
