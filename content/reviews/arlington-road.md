Due to the high marks that *Arlington Road* received from fellow TFM'ers, I
felt obligated to sit down and watch it. I was sorely disappointed. The
overall idea was good, but the plot development was flawed and the casting
was questionable, rendering a potentially great movie little more than
average.

Jeff Bridges plays Michael Faraday, a college professor whose wife was killed
in the line of duty as an FBI agent. Her death motivates him to study
American terrorists and extremist groups. Naturally conspiratorial, Faraday
begins to develop suspicions about his new neighbors the Langs, played by
Tim Robbins and Joan Cusack. As Faraday digs deeper, he discovers his
neighbors are not who they appear to be. Convinced that they have something
planned, he continues to investigate the Langs and their past, discovering
ties with some of the terrorists he has studied. The movie climaxes as
Faraday desperately attempts to expose his neighbors and their secret life.

Perhaps it's my own fault, but I couldn't look at Jeff Bridges without
thinking of "The Dude" in "The Big Lebowski". I felt that he gave a
whining, unstable quality to the character that didn't fit with the plot.
The movie would have been better had he been more rational and professorial
in the role. My biggest complaint is that too much is left unexplained. A
movie with radical and shocking plot twists must strike a delicate balance
between giving adequate explanation in order to be believable, and leaving
enough to the imagination to maintain the surprise element. *Arlington Road*
failed to explain enough. I had too many basic questions of
believability at the end of the movie to be satisfied. It was worth
watching, but not worth the accolades some irrationally exuberant TFM'ers
have given it. My rating 5.5/10.
