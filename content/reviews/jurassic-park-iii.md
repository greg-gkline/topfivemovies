It wasn't that bad. It had a few decent moments. And to be
honest, I *had* wanted to see it, but I'd intended to wait for
the DVD release. But when we arrived at the theater to discover
that the long awaited *American Pie II* was sold out, our choices
came down to *Jurassic Park III* or *America's Sweethearts*.
We chose the former, and I stand by that decision, given the
circumstances.

The most enlightening thing I can say about this movie is that there
was more tension outside the theater between Brian and some big-mouthed
(and equally big-boned) chick than there was on the whole island full
of dinosaurs. The whole movie feels very rushed. There's some token
discussion of raptors at the beginning, mostly just to remind us of
how scared we're supposed to be of them. Yeah, yeah, we get it, they're
smart and scary. Sorry, but no one's got the market on "smart and
scary" cornered like Hannibal Lecter.

It was obviously
the goal of the editing crew to get into the action as quickly as
possible, probably to help cover up the flimsy story. The plot,
by the way, was apparently thrown together from the parts of
Michael Crichton's books that didn't make it into the first two films.
In fact, it wouldn't surprise me if this movie was actually
just footage salvaged from the cutting-room floor of those earlier
flicks, spliced together with a few shots of Tea Leone and a new
raptor that looked an awful lot like Stripe from *Gremlins*.

I also took issue with the soundtrack. John Williams' majestic
*Jurassic Park* score is tossed
in to try and force us into feeling wonderment towards the island
and its inhabitants, but the simple fact is that we've seen it all
before and we're really just waiting to see some blood. The use of
that once grand music here cheapens it immensely.

And speaking of blood, don't blink or you'll miss it. *Scream*
taught us that the death toll is supposed to go up in the sequels,
but I was hard-pressed to find much people-eating carnage here. They
really dropped the ball on that one. The whole time you feel
like you're watching an amusement park ride: some people get bounced
around and scream a lot, but we all know that in the end, they're
going to be just fine.

And speaking of the end, let's not. Frankly, it's pretty dumb.
I'm willing to cut the writers some slack since the rescuers could
have traced the location of the phone, even though that was never
suggested in the movie itself. Basically, though, it's one of those
endings you just accept and feel thankful that it did indeed end.

So, here was a movie made just for the money and to use up the extra
film from the first two movies. But you knew that before you read
this, and so did I. So I can't honestly say I was disappointed.
*Jurassic Park III* is worth a glance, but any more than a glance
will just bore you with same stuff you saw in the first movie.
Mostly, though, it'll make you wish more than ever that all sequels
would be outlawed, and that Sam Neill would find some work that
actually puts his talents to good use.
