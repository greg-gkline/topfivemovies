He's thin and creepy looking to begin with. In films, he always plays the bad guy's sidekick
that seemed to be on his own side the entire time. He was the character
that the good guy can't trust but needs to in order to survive. He always got
his ass kicked but always got away with the goods. Who am I talking about? Not
one, but two of the most gifted character actors of the early and later 20th
century: Peter Lorre and Steve Buscemi. Two of the greatest actors who ever
played crooked thieves.

For those of you who don't know Peter Lorre go to
your local grocery store cereal aisle and pick up a box of Boo-Berry's.
Remember the commercial? That throaty-voiced cartoon ghost is based on Peter
Lorre. Lorre played the side kick to Sydney Greenstreet in *The Maltese Falcon* (1941) and *Casablanca* (1942).
In both films Lorre was the lacky who was
willing to turn on his boss for the right price. Later in his career, Lorre
made a few comedies and did an episode of Alfred Hitchcock's weekly television
show. But Lorre's bad guys of the 1940's film noir were the finest of his
career.

The 1990's brought film noir back. Quentin Tarantino's film
*Reservoir Dogs* (1992) gave the world Mr. Pink, a cockroach-like diamond
thief who gets beat up by Harvey Keitel but lives through a shootout. The
Coen Brothers made *Fargo* (1997) and cast Buscemi as an unlucky bank robber
who gets shot in the face and then goes through a wood chipper.

So what place in the world of film do Lorre and Buscemi have? An important one. The
good guy in a movie is only as good as the villain. The villain is only as good as his
henchmen. If Tom Hanks is the new Jimmy Stewart, then Steve Buscemi is the new encarnation of Peter Lorre. Peter Lorre
is the ancestor of Steve Buscemi. If I had a working Way-Back Machine like Mr. Peabody and his boy
Sherman had and I was to recast *The Maltese Falcon* with Buscemi or bring Lorre to the 1990's and cast him for the role of
Mr. Pink then the same thing would happen. Wonderfully acted creepy characters.
