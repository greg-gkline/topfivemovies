Animated films can be really great, Walt Disney's *Fantasia* showed the
world what only had been seen by hippies on LSD. Nickelodeon, back in the
day, was a great network for children's programing. Who can forget seeing
Alanis Morrisette getting slimed on "You Can't Do That On Television" or
seeing reruns of SCTV.

Nowadays Nickelodeon has become yet another television tyrant, much like
MTV, sodomizing America with boardroom-produced pop culture. How many
times have we seen a flash-in-the-pan pop singer in front of 20 adolescent
kids just to pimp their crappy cd or sweatshop produced merchandise?

The most recent atrocity to the world of animation is *Jimmy Neutron:
Boy Genius*. The writers took a good long 15 minutes to write this one.
Jimmy Neutron is a child inventor who wishes his parents weren't around to
make him follow rules. Jimmy's wish comes true when a fleet of aliens
kidnap all of the neighborhood parents and take them to their home world to
be sacrificed by a giant chicken. Jimmy, his robot dog and a ragtag group
of lovable misfits defy the odds to rescue their parents with inventions
that Jimmy creates.

The film was done with computer animation, which was cool for the first
6 minutes, then got boring. There were only two celebrity voices, Patrick
Stewart was the voice of the not-so-scary despot King Goobot, and Martin
Short played his brother, Oobler.

As far as the soundtrack goes, it could have been the only thing the
movie had going for it if it was used correctly. Tracks by the Ramones, The
Go-Go's and The Backstreet Boys played throughout the film at inopportune
moments. It's a good thing Johnny Ramone is dead, because he would have
gone into an epileptic seizure if he saw these fruity cartoon characters eat
cotton candy and pee in the shower to "Hey Oh Let's Go".

The reason this film got one star is for the animation. This film
couldn't have taken a long time to make, therefore the Gorillaz animated
film can't be that far away. Hopefully there will be a scene where Murdoc
runs Jimmy Neutron over in his Geep. A man can dream, can't he?
