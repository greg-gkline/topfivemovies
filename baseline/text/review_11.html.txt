The Score (2001)
Robert DeNiro, Edward Norton, Marlon Brando
Rating: 8.5 / 10.0
by Greg
(July 14, 2001)
The Score
follows Robert De Niro as an aging master thief on a quest to complete one
final heist before settling down and starting a family with stewardess girlfriend Angela Bassett.
A relatively hackneyed story, and the introduction of a rash and inexperienced partner isn't
particularly new either, even when that partner is played by Ed Norton. And of course, there's
got to be some big cheese financing the whole shebang; in this particular case, the head
honcho is Marlon Brando.
There's a great deal of tension early on as misgivings about this new partner, the motives of his boss,
and the location of the target cause De Niro's character to wonder whether he should continue with
the caper or not. But of course we all know he will (we saw it in the previews), and eventually they
get down to the brass tacks of the planning process.
Up to and including this part, the film's pace is rather slow and methodical, but it doesn't drag
too much. And given the context of De Niro's patient nature, it's expected that such a
perfectionist thief would take his time to get things right. Also keeping things interesting are
Norton's excellent portrayal of his retarded janitor alter ego, and Brando's performance as the
man in charge. Make no mistake: this is not a reprise of the wise and calculating "Godfather".
Brando's character here is more eccentric, and comes across as being somewhat like your Great-Uncle
Louie who's always betting too much money at the track. I wasn't totally thrilled with the
character, but Brando does a good job with what he's given.
Finally the heist unfolds. The complexity of the entry is rivalled only by
Mission Impossible
,
and it would be disappointing to see anything less. The audience is treated to some excellent thrills
as various parts of the plan fall into and/or out of place, and of course there's some tricks and turns and
double-crosses. As I mentioned, the pace of much of the movie is
rather slow and methodical, but in the end this serves to highlight the frantic pace of the escape,
which is well filmed with
NYPD Blue
-like handheld camera shots to increase the feeling of danger
and instability. And the ending is a pleasant surprise which actually elicted applause from the
theater in which I viewed the film.
All in all,
The Score
is a very solid entry in the "big heist" arena and although it shares
many common components with its peers, the performances are top-notch and the overall execution is
sufficient to lend a fresh take on the genre.
Staff Responses
Wow an 8.5! I guess the next movie I see is a toss-up between The Score and My Dog Skip.
