The Village (2004)
Bryce Dallas Howard, Joaquin Phoenix, William Hurt
Rating: 7.5 / 10.0
by Jesse
(July 31, 2004)
As has been thoroughly chronicled here on TopFiveMovies, M. Night
Shyamalan is a cinematic force to be reckoned with. The strength of his
stories, the beauty of his pictures, and the surprising and clever
twists in his plots have quickly turned him into a top Hollywood filmmaker.
In Shyamalan's latest film,
The Village
, his star has dimmed ever so
slightly.
The cast of
The Village
is nearly perfect, led by newcomer Bryce Howard
(the daughter of Ron Howard) and filled out with great performances from
Joaquin Phoenix, William Hurt, and a brilliant performance by Adrien
Brody. Miss Howard was absolutely riveting as Ivy Walker, a young blind
woman who becomes the unwitting centerpiece in the struggle between the
villagers and the strange mythic creatures inhabiting the forbidden
woods at the village's border. Howard and co-star Joaquin Phoenix
strike up an uneasy, innocent, but strong relationship that serves as
the impetus for conflict, change, and resolution.
The only weakness in the cast was Sigourney Weaver who struck me as just
happy to be in a good movie for a change - and not very believable as
Phoenix's mother. But I digress...
Unfortunately, the story let me down.
The
idea
of the story was great. A village of resolute but
threatened idealists squares off against forces real and imagined,
internal and external. The initial execution was great. The
introduction of the villagers and the establishment of the story were
done with grace and subtlety. But, some of the standard Shyamalan
"twists" came way too soon and/or were a bit too predictable - which
robbed the movie's second half of potential drama and tension and robbed
the ultimate revelation of its gravity.
In all,
The Village
was rich and ultimately rewarding, if not a bit
ill-paced.
(Fire away, Shyamalites...)
