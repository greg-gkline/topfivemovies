Snatch (2000)
Brad Pitt, Benicio Del Toro
Rating: 6.5 / 10.0
by Greg
(August 05, 2001)
Snatch
is the fast-paced tale of the fate of a rather
large stolen diamond. Naturally, a rock of such value attracts many
interested parties, each of which tends to maintain possession of the
jewel just long enough to celebrate their victory, before once again
losing it. The film quickly becomes a frantic game of three-card
monte, and the viewer is forced to just sit back and wait to see
where it all ends up.
And that's the primary problem with the movie. There's not a whole lot
of character development, so it's difficult to decide who we should
be rooting for in this tremendously complicated game of cat and mouse.
The first half-hour is especially tough because we have to very quickly
digest 15 or 20 different characters, most of whom speak in a very
thick Cockney accent which further obfuscates their identities and
motivation.
Despite its shortcomings,
Snatch
is extremely well filmed and
edited, and in all honesty the movie probably wouldn't have worked at
all if director Guy Ritchie had done it any other way. It's something
of a mix between a dark comedy and an action film, along the lines of
American Psycho
meets
Reservoir Dogs
. The aforementioned
lack of character development leaves the ending somewhat unfulfilling;
as if we just sat and watched this game of hot potato, but in the end
have nothing to show for it but 100-some minutes of elapsed time.
Still, it's a moderately entertaining film with a few excellent
comedic moments and enough good cinematography to be worth a look.
